The last round escalated on strangeness, which turned out to be the wrong axis — it made the mark weirder without making it truer. So this set changes the question. Each direction takes a system that already exists in the world of money or luck, one with real structure behind it, and lets that structure do the design work. Nothing here is invented to look interesting.

01

正 — Five Strokes

system · counting

01 · the tally

glyph
正 — five strokes
means
five, and “correct”
unicode
U+1D372…376
grid
15 × 13 cells

Across Japan, China, Taiwan and Korea you don’t count in four-bars-and-a-slash. You count in 正, adding one stroke per item until the character is finished at five. Unicode takes this seriously enough to encode every partial state as its own codepoint. Fourteen is written 正正 plus a four-stroke remainder.

So: one stroke per transaction. Five transactions completes a character. A month is a field of them, and the ones that reached five print gold while everything unfinished stays lacquer. The cat is whatever the completed characters happen to spell.

The character also means correct. A ledger that balances is, in the most literal available sense, 正. That pun works in the language rather than in translation, which is the only kind worth building on.

Hanafuda — The Animal Card

A hanafuda deck is twelve suits of four, one suit per month of the year, and the four cards in a suit are ranked bright, animal, ribbon, chaff — twenty points down to one. That is not a metaphor for a year of spending. It is the same data structure.

Your month deals four cards: the bright is the one expense that defined it, the ribbon is the subscriptions, the chaff is the coffees. And the cat is the animal card — ten points, the middle of the deck, the one you actually play for.

Hanafuda cards are 54 × 32 mm. That 1.69:1 is far taller and narrower than any card in any finance app, and it costs nothing to own it.

A daruma is sold blind. You paint the left eye when you set a goal, and it sits on the shelf watching you one-eyed until you finish — then you paint the right. Japanese has a phrase for the moment: both eyes open. Prime ministers do this on camera on election night.

That is a savings goal with a thousand years of grammar already attached, and it beats a progress bar because it is owed rather than merely tracked. The doll is weighted at the base — 七転び八起き, fall down seven times, get up eight. Push the middle one over in your head; it comes back.

Gold isn’t a liberty here. Daruma come in five colours and the gold one is specifically for luck in money — the brand was already using the correct one by accident.

Guilloche — Legal Tender

Every banknote, share certificate and bond coupon you have ever held is covered in guilloche — rosettes cut by a rose engine, a lathe that traces one continuous curve. The maths is an epitrochoid: R and r set the lobe count, a sets how deep they bite. It closes on itself exactly.

This is the only direction here that isn’t about luck. It is about legitimacy — the pattern language a thing wears when it wants to be believed. And because the rosette is a function of three numbers, it can be a function of your three numbers.

The most grown-up option on the page by a wide margin, and the one that would let the brand age past its first funding round without redrawing anything.

Everything above this is about luck. This is the correction. A thermal receipt has no ink at all — a 203 dpi head burns dye in the paper, one colour, no halftone, and the dots drop out when the roll is tired. That dropout is drawn here, deterministically, because a receipt reprints the same.

And then it fades. Thermal print is gone in eighteen months to three years; the slip in your wallet is already disappearing. An app whose whole promise is that your money logs itself has an obvious, unmissable thing to say next to that, and it doesn’t need a cat to say it.

Weakest as a logo, strongest as a campaign. If the brand ever needs one honest, un-charming thing to stand on, it is this — the receipt that doesn’t vanish.

Where this leaves you

  • 01 and 03 are the two real candidates. 正 is the identity — it is a mark, a data visualisation and a pun in the right language all at once. The daruma is the product mechanic — it replaces the savings-goal progress bar with something you owe a second eye to.
  • They compose. Tallies count what you did; the daruma holds what you are counting toward. Nothing in one contradicts the other, and both are gold-and-lacquer on paper.
  • 02 is a system, not a logo. Hanafuda would give you a card grammar, a 1.69:1 proportion and a year view — it earns its place in the UI and never needs to be the app icon.
  • 04 is the adult in the room and the one to keep in the drawer for the day the brand needs to look like it holds money rather than jokes about it.
  • Everything is drawn or generated here. No references, no traces. The tally field, the rosette and the thermal dot matrix all sample one shared silhouette function, and 06-systems.test.mjs pins it.
