Fortune Cat · teardown

Twenty-seven concepts,
and nothing is sacred.

Three rounds of art direction all failed the same way, and it wasn’t the drawing. Every one of them assumed the same five things and then argued only about execution. So this sheet starts by throwing the assumptions out, then explores what is left — twenty-seven concepts across seven territories, each one giving up something the last three rounds treated as load-bearing.

Assumed — a maneki nekoEvery round drew the same beckoning cat in the same pose. Seventeen of the twenty-seven below have no animal in them at all — and every cat that survives is a different cat.
Assumed — gold on creamThree sheets, one palette. There are twenty-seven palettes below and most of them are not warm, not paper, and not gold.
Assumed — JapanRiso, hanafuda, daruma, chops, kanji. A Singapore-built app for anyone’s currency does not owe Japan a costume, and the research round proved the reference can be immaculate and still not fit.
Assumed — there is a markFour concepts here have no logo in any conventional sense: a line, a bracket pair, a gap, and a resident that lives inside the product and never appears in the header.
Assumed — luck is the themeFortune has two meanings and the last three rounds only used one. Several concepts below take the money reading and drop charm entirely.
Kept — the name, and the truthNo bank login. Any currency. Nine dollars once, not forever. It logs itself. Every concept below has to be able to say at least one of those without a paragraph of help.

How to use this

Point at a territory, not a card.

  • This is deliberately unfinished. Twenty-seven sketches at concept fidelity beat three polished plates when the premise is what’s wrong — the last round was the most carefully made and the least right, which is the whole lesson.
  • The territories are the real question. T1 asks whether the mascot survives and T7 assumes it does. T3 asks whether the app is about money or about time. T4 asks whether to fight the spreadsheet or become it. Answer one of those and half these cards die usefully.
  • Some of these contradict each other on purpose. Redaction is confrontational and Envelope is comforting; they cannot both be the brand. That tension is the point of a spread this wide.
  • My three, if you want them: C2 Runway because days-remaining is a truer hero number than a balance and nobody in consumer finance has taken it; B4 Redaction because “no bank login” deserves a visual argument with some teeth in it; and F1 Schrödinger because it is the only one that describes how people actually feel about opening the app. And from T7, G3 Loaf — the cat and the interface primitive being one shape is the only idea on this page that pays rent on every screen.
  • Nothing here is committed to. No file in the app has been touched by any of the three previous rounds either — the whole exploration still lives on a branch.
