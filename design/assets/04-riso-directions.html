Art direction · three ways

The cat, redrawn by hand.

Three riso directions for the Fortune Cat mark. Each is two flat inks, multiplied where they overlap, printed slightly out of register with real paper grain — the way a duplicator actually behaves, not a filter laid over vector art.

On your three references: line art, doodle and naïve are three names for the same neighbourhood, so choosing all three would give you one idea wearing three coats. These three differ structurally instead — line only, line plus texture, shape only. Every one is drawn by hand as vector, so whichever wins is already production art rather than a reference to trace.

The whole body — back, ears, haunch, tail — is one stroke that never lifts, so the gesture reads as one confident movement rather than assembly. The face is drawn separately; a face folded into the same stroke stops being legible, and pretending otherwise is the usual way “one line” logos fail.

The gold sits behind and off-register, doing the job a second plate does on a real duplicator: it doesn't fill the line, it misses it slightly.

Strongest at large sizes, weakest small. A pure contour has nothing left to give up when it shrinks — the 16px test below is the honest answer, not the hero.

The head contour is drawn twice, the second pass faint and not quite tracking the first. That single detail is what separates a doodle from a clean vector pretending to be one — a person searching for the line, not finding it once.

Blue is the nod to your reference sheets, and it is not a departure: the app's dark ground is already #0a0e1c, a blue-black. This makes that relationship explicit.

The warmest of the three, and the most fragile. Every wobble has to be drawn deliberately — and a wobble that lands wrong reads as a mistake, not as charm.

No contour anywhere on the body. The silhouette does the work, the edges are cut slightly wonky, and the muzzle is a hole — paper removed, so the sheet colour shows through. On a real duplicator that is the only white you get.

Vermilion is on the collar, seal and coin only, never on the cat. Your rule survives intact, and the red finally has somewhere honest to live.

The only one that holds at 16px — a filled shape loses detail gracefully where a contour just disappears. Also the biggest departure from what you ship today.

Where this leaves you

  • These are drawings, not references. Every path is hand-authored vector, so whichever you pick is already the production mark — no tracing step, no generation, nothing to redraw.
  • All three carry the three moods, because the cat's face is a pure function of net cash flow in lib/catState. A style that can't hold saving / break-even / in-the-red isn't a candidate, whatever it looks like at hero size.
  • This is a real break from the gold-leaf mark. Riso and lacquer-gold are different promises. Picking one of these means the dashboard cat changes too, or the landing page and the app stop looking related.
  • Combining is available. R3's silhouette with R2's spark ticks is the obvious hybrid — shape that survives small, doodle warmth where there's room for it.
